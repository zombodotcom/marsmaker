MarsMaker

Drive a dune buggy at 1:1 scale across real Mars terrain, made from NASA HiRISE stereo elevation models (1 m per post, downsampled for the web). Pick a site and hit Play. It runs in your browser, with nothing to install.

Options

Best on a desktop or laptop with a keyboard and a decent GPU. Phones get on-screen touch buttons, but the terrain is heavy, so some phones will struggle. Each site downloads 5-30 MB, plus about 40 MB for the engine the first time.

Controls

W↑ throttle
S↓ reverse
AD←→ steer
Space brake
R flip upright
C chase / high camera
Shift boost
CtrlJ jump
in the air: WS pitch, AD roll
M switch FUN / REAL mode

FUN (default) has extra grip and power, Shift boost, jumps, air control and auto-flip. REAL is loose dry sand with the real power and no assists, so boost, jump and air control do nothing. Press M while driving to switch.

Click the terrain once so it gets keyboard focus. The page freezes for a few seconds while a site decodes, which is normal. You spawn in the most dune-rich part of each site. Black areas are gaps in the NASA elevation model.

Where this comes from

Each site is a HiRISE Digital Terrain Model: a stereo pair from the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter turned into an elevation grid by the University of Arizona and USGS, with the matching orthoimage draped on top. MarsMaker converts those into game-engine tiles (Unreal, Unity, Godot, Bevy). This page runs the Bevy viewer compiled to WebAssembly on WebGL2.

Imagery and elevation: HiRISE, NASA/JPL/University of Arizona (DTMs also USGS), public domain / CC0, via the HiRISE DTM archive and the USGS Astrogeology analysis-ready data.
